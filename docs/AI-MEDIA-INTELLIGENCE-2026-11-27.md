## Executive assessment

Argentina's media environment is not simply moving from television to streaming. It is becoming a layered attention market in which legacy broadcasters, digital publishers, creator-led channels, social platforms and AI-assisted discovery coexist. The strategic competition is increasingly about distribution, repeat attention, credibility and monetization—not only about who publishes first.

The strongest current evidence points to four structural signals:

1. **News trust is under pressure.** The Reuters Institute's 2026 Argentina report places overall trust in news at 26%, down six percentage points year over year and the lowest level in the report's decade-long series. It reports that 46% sometimes or often avoid the news and that only 10% pay for online news.
2. **Video and creator-led formats are becoming more important news gateways.** The Reuters Institute describes livestreaming and online video as increasingly prominent, particularly for younger audiences. This is a format shift, not proof that every streaming channel has mass reach.
3. **Audience measurement needs careful interpretation.** October 2026 channel-view estimates published by different ranking services do not match exactly. Views, live concurrent viewers, unique users and news reach are different metrics and must never be combined into one ranking.
4. **Ownership is changing while transparency remains incomplete.** Telefe's sale to a Scaglione-led local holding in 2025 and reported control changes involving Blender and Carajo illustrate consolidation and investment in audiovisual distribution. The public evidence does not justify treating every reported commercial relationship as a fully verified corporate-control map.

**Strategic implication:** VILANOVA AI MEDIA should sell clarity about the attention system: who distributes content, which formats are growing, how audiences are measured, where ownership is documented, and where evidence remains incomplete.

## 2. Current media and platform trends

### 2.1 Trust and willingness to pay

The Reuters Institute reports:

- Overall trust in news: **26%**, down 6 percentage points year over year.
- News avoidance: **46%** sometimes or often avoid the news.
- Online news payment: **10%**.
- Around eight in ten respondents use the internet for news weekly.
- Almost one in ten respondents use AI chatbots for news, up four percentage points from 2025.

These figures suggest that reach alone is an incomplete commercial measure. Media brands and creators need to establish repeat attention and perceived credibility while competing in a crowded, low-trust environment.

### 2.2 Streaming is part of the news ecosystem

The Reuters Institute describes livestreamed talk formats that combine commentary, interviews, news and entertainment as an increasingly important information source, especially among younger users. The format can lower some of the fixed production costs associated with traditional television, while making distribution and creator identity central to the product.

### 2.3 October 2026 YouTube channel-view signals

A 5 November 2026 report by Cultura Geek, citing En Directo Stream's October ranking, reported the following monthly views for selected native streaming channels:

| Channel | Reported October 2026 views | Reported month-on-month change |
|---|---:|---:|
| OLGA | 99,495,621 | +21.40% |
| LUZU TV | 95,310,181 | −10.38% |
| Blender | 30,376,363 | +14.08% |
| Gelatina | 26,288,010 | +17.26% |

These are **accumulated YouTube views for the reported month**, not unique viewers, average concurrent live viewers, subscribers, or verified news reach. The source is secondary reporting of a third-party ranking.

A separate October 2026 YouTube Rating page reports different monthly totals for some of the same channels. This discrepancy is a warning against treating third-party channel rankings as interchangeable. Before publishing a leaderboard, VILANOVA AI MEDIA should record the provider, collection window, channel inclusion rules and metric definition.

**Confidence:** Medium for the figures as attributed to the named ranking source; low for cross-provider comparability.

## 3. Media groups, concentration and ownership

### 3.1 Structural baseline: Media Ownership Monitor Argentina

The Media Ownership Monitor (MOM) Argentina concentration study is a **2018 baseline**, not a current 2026 market-share measurement. Its findings include:

- The four leading print-media business groups accounted for 74.81% of sales in the study.
- Grupo Clarín alone accounted for 43% of the print market in that historical dataset.
- The four leading radio groups accounted for 53% of audience in the study.
- The four leading television groups accounted for 56.7% of the measured Buenos Aires market.
- MOM's temporary cross-media concentration indicator attributed 59.24% of the measured concentration to eight leading groups, with Grupo Clarín at 25.28%, Grupo América at 7.25%, Viacom at 7.10%, Grupo Indalo at 6.62% and PRISA/Albavisión at 6.21%.

These values must not be presented as current market shares. Their continuing value is structural: the study documents how ownership across print, radio, television and online outlets can create cross-media agenda-setting power.

MOM also notes that corporate structures are not always directly and transparently accessible to the public. Its company directory identifies groups including Clarín, América, Indalo, Infobae, La Nación, Perfil, Octubre, Olmos, PRISA, TeleCentro, Televisión Litoral, Kuarzo and others.

### 3.2 Telefe: return to local ownership

Forbes reported on 23 October 2025 that Paramount sold Telefe to a local holding led by Gustavo Scaglione and José Luis Manzano. The report describes the transaction as a significant shift from international to local ownership. Other Argentine reporting has discussed the wider business consortium around the transaction; the exact corporate relationships should be checked against current company filings before being represented as a definitive group chart.

**Editorial rule:** describe the transaction and attribute the account to its source. Do not infer unreported beneficial ownership or governance rights.

### 3.3 Blender and Carajo

Letra P reported on 28 May 2025 that Augusto Marini's Cale Group took control of Blender and Carajo. The report describes the transaction as a control change within the existing ownership circle and says the channels would continue their day-to-day operations. This is a journalistic account, not a substitute for a current corporate registry extract or shareholder filing.

### 3.4 Gelatina

Gelatina's public YouTube channel page displays a 2026 programme schedule and current publishing activity. This confirms an active channel and programming presence; it does **not** establish its ownership, financing, audience composition or political affiliation.

## 6. Emerging trends and hypotheses

These are evidence-informed hypotheses, not measured forecasts.

1. **Creator-led news distribution may keep expanding.** The Reuters Institute's 2026 findings on livestreaming and creators support continued monitoring of host-led formats, clips and community-driven distribution.
2. **Trust may become a sharper differentiator than raw reach.** A 26% overall trust score alongside persistent brand-level differences suggests a commercial opportunity for transparent sourcing, recognizable expertise and accountable editorial practice.
3. **AI-mediated discovery will matter more.** The reported rise to 9% of respondents using AI chatbots for news is still a minority behavior, but it is a measurable increase worth tracking.
4. **Streaming businesses may consolidate or professionalize.** Reported control changes involving Blender and Carajo, together with the broader investment in streaming formats, support monitoring of ownership, sponsorship and monetization structures.
5. **Measurement disputes will become a product issue.** Conflicting third-party channel totals show why brands and agencies need standardized metric definitions before making sponsorship decisions.

## 8. Methodology and limitations

- Research cut-off: 27 November 2026.
- Sources were reviewed as publicly available web pages and search-indexed channel information.
- The Reuters Institute survey is a structured audience study; it is not a real-time social-listening feed.
- MOM concentration figures are historical 2018 measurements and are labeled accordingly.
- Streaming rankings are secondary reports of third-party measurements; their methods may differ.
- Ownership claims from journalism are attributed and are not treated as registry-confirmed beneficial ownership.
- Hashtag candidates are not measured rankings.
- No sentiment, unique reach, live concurrent audience, sponsorship contract or influencer score has been fabricated.
- No platform account, paid provider, social-listening API or model API is connected.
